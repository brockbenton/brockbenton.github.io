OAP

An onchain attendance protocol on Base with QR check-in and gasless, soulbound ERC-1155 tokens

Overview

OAP (Onchain Attendance Protocol) replaces sign-in sheets at CU Blockchain meetings. Members scan a QR code at the meeting and receive a non-transferable ERC-1155 token for that session. Minting is gasless, so members don’t need ETH or any crypto experience to check in.

Attendance stats, semester rollups, member tiers, and the admin dashboard are all computed from these tokens. Since the tokens can’t be transferred, a member can’t give their attendance to someone else.

OAP is a work in progress and is currently deployed to Base Sepolia.

How It Works

High level. An admin creates a session, which is written onchain. At the meeting, the admin displays a QR code that the backend has signed and that expires after a few minutes. A member signs in with email, Google, or a passkey, scans the code, and signs a check-in message. The backend verifies both signatures and queues a mint. A relay wallet pays the gas and mints the token to the member.

Low level: the check-in loop.

  1. Create session. createSession on the AttendanceRegistry contract is restricted to ADMIN_ROLE and stores the session’s date, name, and semester. The backend only accepts check-ins once that transaction confirms.
  2. Sign the QR. The backend builds a payload of sessionId, issuedAt, expiresAt (10 minutes by default), and a random nonce, then signs it with the admin key. The QR code holds the payload and its signature.
  3. Check in. The member’s client signs I am checking into session {id} as {address} at {timestamp}. The server rejects signatures older than 60 seconds, verifies the QR’s admin signature and expiry, and recovers the signer’s address to confirm it matches the member’s logged-in wallet. A forwarded screenshot of an expired QR or a reused signature gets rejected.
  4. Record. A unique (member, session) constraint in PostgreSQL rejects duplicate check-ins before anything is sent onchain.
  5. Mint. A BullMQ job calls mint from the relay wallet, which holds RELAY_ROLE. Jobs retry three times with exponential backoff, and the worker processes one job at a time to avoid nonce collisions.
  6. Reconcile. Before minting, and again after a final failure, the job checks the member’s onchain balance. If an earlier attempt already minted the token, the check-in is marked confirmed instead of minting again or being marked failed.

The contract only enforces the rules that have to hold regardless of the backend: only the relay can mint, only into an active session, at most one token per member per session, and no transfers. QR signing, identity checks, and stats are handled offchain, so they can change without redeploying the contract.

What I Built

Contract: AttendanceRegistry.sol, written in Solidity and tested with Foundry.

  • ERC-1155 with OpenZeppelin AccessControl (ADMIN_ROLE, RELAY_ROLE) and ReentrancyGuard
  • Soulbound tokens: _update reverts on anything other than a mint, and setApprovalForAll always reverts
  • Session lifecycle (createSession, closeSession) with explicit errors (SessionDoesNotExist, SessionNotActive, AlreadyCheckedIn, Soulbound)
  • Token metadata served from IPFS through uri()
  • 45 Foundry tests

Backend: Node, Express, and TypeScript.

  • QR signing and verification, member signature checks, and rate-limited check-in endpoints
  • The BullMQ mint relay, with jobs that are safe to retry and that check onchain state before minting
  • PostgreSQL and Prisma schema for sessions, members, and check-ins
  • Stats: all-time and per-semester attendance, streaks, and tiers (75% or higher attendance makes you an Official Member)

Frontend: Next.js with Privy login, a camera QR scanner, member stats and a token vault, a leaderboard, and an admin dashboard for running sessions.

GitHub Actions runs the contract tests, backend build and tests, and frontend build on every PR.

Stack

  • Onchain: Solidity and Foundry on Base, OpenZeppelin
  • Backend: Node.js, Express, TypeScript, PostgreSQL and Prisma, Redis and BullMQ, viem
  • Frontend: Next.js 14, Privy, wagmi and viem, Tailwind
  • Storage: IPFS for token metadata
